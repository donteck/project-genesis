# Canonical Blueprint 046

## Platform Governance & Constitutional Runtime Architecture

## Purpose

Define the complete architecture through which Project Genesis constitutional authority, canonical principles, platform policies, institutional rules, human approvals, exceptions, amendments, appeals, evidence, and accountability become enforceable and explainable within LearningOS.

Blueprint 046 establishes the governance control plane above the LearningOS Kernel. Blueprint 045 defined the smallest authoritative platform core. Blueprint 046 defines how legitimate governance directs that core without allowing code, administrators, organizations, extensions, workflows, or AI agents to invent authority outside the Project Genesis constitutional order.

All future governance, policy, constitutional enforcement, amendment, exception, appeal, emergency authority, and machine-enforceable institutional rule systems must build upon this blueprint rather than create independent governance runtimes.

---

## 1. The Constitutional Runtime Principle

LearningOS must be governed by more than configuration and code.

The platform exists beneath a hierarchy of purpose, constitutional commitments, canonical blueprints, policies, standards, and accountable human decisions. The Constitutional Runtime makes that hierarchy operational while preserving the distinction between moral authority, institutional governance, and technical enforcement.

It must identify the authority behind every enforceable rule, preserve the meaning and scope of governing sources, translate approved policy into deterministic controls, explain decisions, require human judgment where rules cannot decide legitimately, preserve exceptions and appeals, and prevent lower authority from weakening higher obligations.

> **Code may enforce a rule, but code must never become the source of constitutional authority.**

---

## 2. Governance Philosophy

LearningOS follows these principles:

- **Authority before enforcement:** Every policy traces to an approved source, accountable owner, lawful scope, and effective period.
- **Human legitimacy:** Automation supports governance but does not replace accountable human authority in amendments, appeals, exceptions, or high-impact decisions.
- **Higher rules constrain lower rules:** Organization, tenant, product, workflow, extension, and agent policy cannot contradict mandatory constitutional commitments.
- **Explanation is part of the decision:** A decision without authority, inputs, obligations, and review path is incomplete.
- **Governance is versioned:** Policies, interpretations, exceptions, and amendments have explicit versions and effective dates.
- **Exceptions are governed:** An exception is scoped, justified, approved, expiring, and auditable.
- **Rights survive automation:** Automation cannot erase notice, accessibility, privacy, review, appeal, or other protected interests.

> **A trusted platform can explain not only what it decided, but why it had the right to decide.**

The Constitutional Runtime defines legitimate policy and evaluates it. The Kernel supplies trusted context and enforces decisions and obligations.

Governance cannot bypass Kernel security. The Kernel cannot silently rewrite governance meaning.

## 4. Authority Hierarchy

LearningOS recognizes this ordered authority:

```text
Project Genesis Charter
        ↓
Permanent Constitutions
        ↓
Canonical Blueprints
        ↓
Engineering and Governance Standards
        ↓
Platform Policies
        ↓
Enterprise and Tenant Policies
        ↓
Domain and Product Policies
        ↓
Workflow, Extension, and Agent Rules
```

Each layer derives authority from the layer above it and operates only within delegated scope.

Conflicts are resolved using authority level, mandatory status, jurisdiction, scope, effective time, delegation, and formal interpretation. Lower policy cannot override a higher mandatory rule merely because it is newer or more specific.

---

## 5. Constitutional Source Registry

Every governing source receives a durable record containing source identity, type, title, version, authority level, owner, approval evidence, scope, mandatory status, effective period, supersession, canonical location, content digest, and interpretation status.

GitHub remains the canonical source of truth for Project Genesis architecture. Runtime registries distribute approved, immutable governance releases.

Changing a source requires a new version or formal amendment. Runtime operators cannot edit constitutional text through administrative configuration.

---

## 6. Policy Architecture

Policies may govern learner rights, identity, organizations, tenants, privacy, security, learning, assessment, credentials, AI agents, extensions, packages, workflows, content, accessibility, commerce, analytics, operations, and governance itself.

Every policy defines identity, version, title, authority sources, owner, scope, subjects, resources, actions, conditions, effect, obligations, exceptions, review authority, appeal path, effective period, and status.

Effects may allow, deny, require approval, require notice, require consent, restrict data, impose limits, or require evidence.

Policies distinguish human-readable intent from machine-evaluable rules. Neither representation may contradict the other.

---

## 7. Policy Lifecycle

```text
Proposed
    ↓
Authored
    ↓
Validated
    ↓
Impact Assessed
    ↓
Reviewed
    ↓
Approved
    ↓
Published
    ↓
Effective
    ↓
Amended, Superseded, or Retired
```

Every transition records actor, authority, evidence, reason, time, and resulting version. Draft or unapproved policy cannot become runtime authority.

Policy authoring includes plain-language purpose, authority citations, scope, machine-readable conditions, review criteria, obligations, remedies, examples, test cases, effective dates, and appeal paths.

AI may assist authoring and analysis. AI-generated policy remains a draft until authorized humans approve it.

---

## 8. Policy Compiler and Registry

The compiler transforms approved policy definitions into signed, immutable runtime artifacts.

Compilation validates source authority and integrity, schema, scope, delegation, conflicts with higher rules, undefined terms, determinism, supported obligations, explanations, tests, compatibility, and effective dates.

It produces a policy bundle, authority map, decision tests, human-readable summary, digest, signature, and compatibility declaration.

Published bundles are immutable and versioned. The registry preserves approval evidence, test evidence, effective periods, supersession, revocation, and distribution history.

Runtime nodes verify signature, digest, compatibility, and effective status before activation. Distribution supports canaries, staged rollout, rollback, regional controls, offline verification, and emergency revocation.

---

## 9. Constitutional Decision Runtime

```text
Verified Identity and Tenant Context
                +
Requested Capability and Resource
                +
Applicable Authority Graph
                +
Effective Policy Bundle
                +
Runtime Conditions and Evidence
                =
Decision, Obligations, Explanation, and Review Path
```

The runtime returns allow, deny, require action, require approval, or indeterminate. Indeterminate never silently becomes allow; it fails safely or routes to authorized human review.

Consequential decision records preserve request, actor and tenant context, capability, resource reference, policy version, authority sources, material inputs, result, obligations, explanation, review path, and time.

Historical decisions remain interpretable under the policy effective when they occurred.

---

## 10. Obligations and Human Approval

Decisions may require step-up authentication, human approval, notice, consent, data minimization, masking, purpose restriction, rate or cost limits, geographic restriction, retention, additional audit, or post-action review.

The Kernel confirms mandatory obligations before protected execution completes. Obligations are not optional interface guidance.

Human approval is required when constitutional meaning needs interpretation, rights or high-impact interests are affected, policy requires judgment, an exception or appeal exists, emergency authority is invoked, or governing sources change.

Approval records identify approver, authority, scope, evidence, decision, conditions, time, and conflicts of interest. No person approves beyond delegated authority.

---

## 11. Exceptions, Appeals, and Remedies

Exceptions are scoped, justified, approved, time-bound, non-transferable unless stated, and auditable. They identify the policy, requester, beneficiary, scope, evidence, approver, authority, conditions, effective period, review date, and revocation state.

No exception overrides an authority that does not permit exceptions. Repeated exceptions trigger policy review.

Affected people and organizations receive appropriate notice, explanation, applicable policy, evidence-submission opportunity, independent or elevated review, timely resolution, remedy, and audit history.

Successful appeals correct downstream records and outcomes where possible. Appeals protect sensitive security information while still enabling meaningful review.

---

## 12. Amendments and Constitutional Change

Constitutional sources change only through their authorized governance process.

An amendment defines the source affected, proposed language or interpretation, authority to amend, rationale, evidence, impact, approval, effective date, transition, and historical preservation.

Runtime policy updates follow only after the canonical source is approved and committed. Prior versions remain available for historical decisions. Amendment does not rewrite the past.

Unresolved conflicts between authority sources become indeterminate decisions requiring authorized interpretation; the runtime cannot invent constitutional meaning through arbitrary rule ordering.

---

## 13. Enterprise, Tenant, and Ecosystem Governance

Organizations may create policies within Blueprint 043 authority and Blueprint 044 isolation. They may strengthen security, privacy, approval, marketplace, agent, content, or learning controls, but cannot weaken mandatory Project Genesis rules.

Tenant policy remains isolated in storage, distribution, caching, evaluation, audit, and administration.

Blueprints 039–042 remain authoritative for workflows, extensions, packages, and AI agents. Governance defines permitted capabilities, permissions, data, tools, approvals, evidence, audit, suspension, and appeal paths.

Extensions and agents cannot register constitutional sources, modify policy bundles, approve their own authority, or conceal actions. Marketplace popularity, payment, certification, or first-party status never grants constitutional privilege.

---

## 14. Kernel Integration

Canonical Blueprint 045 remains authoritative for enforcement.

```text
Governance Authority
        ↓

        ↓
Constitutional Decision Runtime
        ↓
Kernel Enforcement Point
        ↓

```

The Kernel supplies verified context and enforces decisions. Governance supplies authority, policy meaning, obligations, and explanation.

Kernel code cannot silently override effective constitutional policy. Governance cannot request a capability the Kernel does not safely expose.

## 15. Emergency Governance

Emergency authority may address active security threats, safety risks, unlawful behavior, critical availability incidents, or compromised governance systems.

Actions may suspend a capability, revoke a bundle, disable an extension or agent, restrict data, freeze a tenant operation, require manual approval, or activate a safe platform mode.

Emergency authority is predefined, least-privilege, time-bound, logged, and subject to mandatory post-incident review. Emergency status cannot become a permanent way to avoid ordinary governance.

---

## 16. Transparency, Audit, and Evidence

LearningOS publishes appropriate constitutional sources, public policies, change summaries, effective dates, material automated-decision categories, appeal paths, transparency reports, and safe emergency summaries.

Governance audit covers source registration, policy authorship, approval, compilation, activation, decisions, obligations, human approvals, exceptions, appeals, amendments, emergency action, and overrides.

Records identify actor, authority, action, target, reason, policy version, time, correlation, and outcome. They are append-oriented, access-controlled, tenant-aware, and protected from the systems they audit.

Transparency must be understandable, not merely technically available.

---

## 17. Security and Reliability

The runtime requires strong identity, separation of duties, signed sources and bundles, protected keys, least privilege, tenant isolation, integrity verification, secure distribution, rollback protection, rate limits, audit, anomaly detection, and break-glass governance.

Policy injection, unauthorized downgrade, stale replay, signature substitution, hidden override, and tenant leakage are critical threats.

Reliability uses replicated registries, content-addressed bundles, verified caches, deterministic evaluation, bounded latency, staged activation, rapid revocation, disaster recovery, and historical replay.

Mandatory authority, tenant, identity, integrity, and rights protections fail closed when they cannot be verified.

---

## 18. Observability

The runtime exposes evaluation count and latency, decision outcomes, bundle distribution and activation, integrity failures, conflicts, approval latency, exception volume and expiration, appeal outcomes, emergency use, rollback, and tenant governance health.

Operators can identify the active bundle and authority graph for every runtime instance.

Metrics support accountability without exposing private decision inputs or enabling surveillance.

---

## 19. Versioning and Testing

Constitutional sources, policy schemas, bundles, decision contracts, and Kernel interfaces are independently versioned with declared compatibility, migration, mixed-version rollout, deprecation, and removal rules.

A new policy version cannot retroactively change the authority or explanation of a historical decision.

Testing includes authority hierarchy, compiler, conflicts, deterministic decisions, obligations, approvals, exceptions, appeals, amendments, tenant isolation, ecosystem governance, Kernel integration, signatures, downgrade attacks, emergency controls, compatibility, rollback, failure injection, recovery, and performance.

Every policy release includes positive, negative, boundary, exception, and explanation cases.

---

## 20. Governance of Governance

The Constitutional Runtime is itself governed.

Changes to authority ordering, policy semantics, compiler behavior, decision contracts, emergency powers, appeals, or audit receive constitutional and security review proportional to impact.

AI may assist analysis, drafting, simulation, conflict detection, and explanation. AI cannot approve constitutional change or become the final interpreter of ambiguous authority.

Regular review examines effectiveness, unintended consequences, exception patterns, appeal outcomes, disparate impact, operational burden, and alignment with Project Genesis purpose.

## 23. Governance Anti-Patterns

LearningOS must reject:

- **Code as constitution:** Treating implementation behavior as governing authority.
- **Policy without source:** Enforcing rules without legitimate authority.
- **Admin supremacy:** Letting administrators bypass constitutional constraints.
- **AI interpretation authority:** Letting a model decide ambiguous constitutional meaning.
- **Silent override:** Hiding exceptions or precedence.
- **Permanent emergency:** Using emergency powers as ordinary governance.
- **Exception by habit:** Bypassing policy instead of reviewing it.
- **Appeal theater:** Offering review without independence or remedy.
- **Retroactive rewriting:** Applying new policy meaning to historical decisions.
- **Unsigned policy:** Executing unverified governance artifacts.
- **Tenant policy leakage:** Applying one organization's governance to another.
- **Unexplainable denial:** Producing consequential outcomes without authority or review paths.

---

## Final Principle

LearningOS must be powerful enough to evolve and disciplined enough to remember why it exists.

The Charter gives purpose. The Constitutions establish commitments. The Canonical Blueprints define architecture. Policies translate authority into action. The Constitutional Runtime makes those actions consistent, explainable, reviewable, and accountable. The Kernel enforces the boundary, but human stewardship preserves legitimacy.

> **The platform is governed when its power remains answerable to its principles.**
