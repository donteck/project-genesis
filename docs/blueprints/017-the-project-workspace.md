## Purpose and Authority

The Project Workspace is the environment in which a learner turns knowledge into an artifact, decisions into evidence, feedback into improvement, and experience into a story of capability. It connects research, planning, architecture, implementation, testing, documentation, reflection, portfolio evidence, and publication around one durable project record.

The workspace is not limited to software. A project may be a business plan, lesson, research study, design, community initiative, media work, experiment, process, product, performance, or another artifact through which capability can be practiced and demonstrated.

This blueprint defines the workspace philosophy, lifecycle, eight modules, AI Mentor responsibilities, documentation, portfolio generation, publication, and product and engineering requirements that apply across LearningOS.

> "Projects transform knowledge into evidence."

---

## 1. Why Projects Matter

Projects require knowledge to survive contact with reality. They ask learners to define a problem, make choices, work within constraints, combine concepts, create something reviewable, respond to evidence, and explain the result.

Projects matter because they:

- Transform passive understanding into applied capability
- Reveal gaps that content completion may conceal
- Develop judgment under uncertainty
- Integrate skills that are often taught separately
- Produce authentic material for feedback and revision
- Make learning visible through artifacts and decisions
- Build confidence grounded in completed work
- Create credible evidence for assessment and portfolios
- Give learners something useful to share or contribute
- Preserve lessons that can help future builders

A project is not automatically meaningful because it produces an artifact. It should connect to a defined purpose, intended capability, appropriate challenge, evidence criteria, and opportunity for reflection.

Projects should allow recoverable failure. Unexpected results, changed assumptions, rejected approaches, and incomplete attempts can become powerful evidence of learning when documented honestly and followed by reflection or revision.

> "Creation is the proof of learning."

## 2. The Workspace Philosophy

The Project Workspace should make disciplined creation easier without reducing creation to a rigid checklist.

Its governing principles are:

### Purpose before Output

Every project begins with a problem, opportunity, question, audience, or contribution. The workspace should keep that purpose visible as implementation choices accumulate.

### Process Is Evidence

The final artifact matters, but research, decisions, iterations, tests, feedback, and reflection reveal how capability developed and whether the learner understands the result.

### One Project, One Durable Record

Every module should contribute to a shared, versioned project identity rather than create disconnected documents and duplicate artifacts.

### Guidance without Dependence

Templates, AI, mentors, rubrics, and examples should help the learner make better decisions while preserving authorship, productive struggle, and independent capability.

### Revision Is Part of Building

The workspace should treat feedback, testing, correction, and changed understanding as expected parts of creation—not as evidence that the project failed.

### Documentation Is Created with the Work

Important reasoning should be captured when decisions occur. Documentation should emerge throughout the lifecycle rather than depend on memory at the end.

### Evidence Should Travel

With appropriate permissions, project artifacts, decisions, feedback, and reflection should become portable portfolio evidence and reusable knowledge.

### Publication Is Deliberate

Sharing requires review of quality, privacy, rights, accessibility, safety, attribution, and audience. Completion does not imply automatic publication.

## 3. The Project Lifecycle

The canonical project lifecycle is:

```text
Idea
  ↓
Research
  ↓
Planning
  ↓
Architecture
  ↓
Implementation
  ↓
Testing
  ↓
Reflection
  ↓
Portfolio
  ↓
Publication
```

### Idea

The learner identifies a problem, question, opportunity, audience, or contribution and expresses why it matters.

### Research

The learner investigates context, prior work, sources, people, constraints, risks, and evidence before committing to a solution.

### Planning

The learner defines scope, intended outcome, requirements, milestones, resources, ownership, and measures of completion.

### Architecture

The learner organizes the solution: structure, components, relationships, decisions, tradeoffs, interfaces, methods, or conceptual model.

### Implementation

The learner creates the artifact through visible iterations, decisions, collaboration, and disciplined execution.

### Testing

The learner compares the work with requirements, evidence, users, rubrics, quality standards, and expected behavior, then records and addresses findings.

### Reflection

The learner interprets outcomes, feedback, failures, growth, changed assumptions, and transferable lessons.

### Portfolio

The learner curates credible evidence and tells an honest story of purpose, process, capability, and result.

### Publication

The learner shares the approved artifact and its appropriate context with a defined audience, or records a deliberate decision to keep it private.

The lifecycle is iterative. Testing may return the learner to architecture or implementation. Research may continue throughout the project. Reflection belongs at milestones as well as completion. Publication may generate new evidence and begin another revision.

## 4. The Eight Workspace Modules

### Module I — Overview

The Overview provides project identity and orientation.

It contains:

- Title, summary, purpose, and intended audience
- Owning learner, team, organization, and journey
- Intended capabilities and evidence requirements
- Current phase, status, progress, and next action
- Milestones, due dates, collaborators, and permissions
- Recent changes, feedback, blockers, and attention items

### Module II — Research

Research preserves inquiry and evidence before and during creation.

It contains:

- Research questions and assumptions
- Sources, citations, notes, and annotations
- User, stakeholder, market, or contextual findings
- Examples, precedents, and competing approaches
- Risks, constraints, unknowns, and evidence quality
- AI-assisted research with provenance and verification state

### Module III — Plan & Architecture

Plan & Architecture turns purpose and research into an executable design.

It contains:

- Scope, requirements, acceptance criteria, and exclusions
- Milestones, tasks, estimates, dependencies, and ownership
- Diagrams, models, outlines, flows, and interfaces
- Architecture decisions and tradeoffs
- Accessibility, privacy, security, ethical, and maintenance considerations
- Change history and approved revisions

### Module IV — Build

Build is the primary creation surface and artifact hub.

It contains:

- Drafts, files, code, media, documents, designs, or linked external artifacts
- Versions, branches, iterations, and change summaries
- Tasks and current implementation focus
- Collaboration, comments, review requests, and handoffs
- Save, sync, conflict, and integration state
- Connections to tools appropriate to the project type

### Module V — Test & Review

Test & Review compares the work with intended outcomes and quality criteria.

It contains:

- Test plans, cases, rubrics, and validation criteria
- Automated and manual results
- User, peer, mentor, educator, and stakeholder feedback
- Defects, findings, severity, and resolution state
- Revisions linked to evidence
- Approval, reassessment, and exception records

### Module VI — Documentation & Reflection

Documentation & Reflection preserves what the project teaches.

It contains:

- Project narrative and operating guidance
- Decision, architecture, setup, and maintenance records
- Learning journal and milestone reflections
- Failures, corrections, and changed assumptions
- Contributor recognition and source attribution
- Lessons, reusable knowledge, and questions for future builders

### Module VII — Portfolio

Portfolio converts authentic project evidence into a learner-controlled presentation of capability.

It contains:

- Selected artifact versions and media
- Purpose, role, audience, and constraints
- Process, decisions, iterations, and contribution
- Evidence mapped to capabilities and criteria
- Feedback, test results, outcomes, and reflection
- Visibility, export, verification, and sharing controls

### Module VIII — Publication

Publication prepares and distributes the work to an intended audience.

It contains:

- Publication target, audience, format, and channel
- Content and artifact release candidate
- Quality, accessibility, privacy, security, rights, and attribution checks
- Reviewer approvals and unresolved exceptions
- Release notes, version, date, and canonical link
- Update, correction, withdrawal, and archive controls

Modules may adapt to project type and role, but the underlying responsibilities should remain recognizable. A simple project may present them as a guided sequence; a complex team project may expose them as full workspaces.

## 5. AI Mentor Responsibilities

The AI Mentor supports the learner throughout the project lifecycle while protecting authorship, assessment integrity, and independent capability.

### Idea and Research

The Mentor may help clarify the problem, identify assumptions, formulate research questions, suggest search strategies, compare sources, and reveal missing perspectives. It should distinguish sourced findings from generated possibilities.

### Planning and Architecture

The Mentor may help define scope, requirements, milestones, risks, alternatives, and decision criteria. It should ask the learner to make and explain consequential choices rather than silently make them.

### Implementation

The Mentor may explain techniques, provide examples, diagnose obstacles, suggest incremental steps, and review drafts. Assistance should be proportional to the capability the learner is expected to develop.

### Testing

The Mentor may help derive tests, identify edge cases, compare work with criteria, interpret failures, and propose validation methods. It should not fabricate test results or claim that unexecuted checks passed.

### Reflection and Documentation

The Mentor may ask reflective questions, organize learner-approved notes, identify lessons, and help improve clarity. It must preserve the learner's voice and label AI-generated drafts.

### Portfolio and Publication

The Mentor may help select evidence, explain capability, improve presentation, perform readiness checks, and identify privacy, rights, accessibility, or attribution issues. It should not exaggerate contribution or outcomes.

Across all modules, the Mentor should use the project purpose, current phase, learner journey, permitted artifacts, and rubric as context. It should end assistance with a meaningful action the learner can own.

## 6. Documentation System

The documentation system converts project activity into durable knowledge without forcing the learner to reconstruct the entire process at completion.

### Documentation Layers

#### Project Definition

Purpose, audience, scope, requirements, capabilities, evidence expectations, constraints, and intended outcome.

#### Research Record

Questions, sources, provenance, findings, uncertainty, notes, permissions, and conclusions.

#### Decision Record

Important choices, alternatives, evidence, tradeoffs, owner, date, and conditions that may require revision.

#### Build Record

Versions, milestones, changes, contributors, linked artifacts, dependencies, and implementation notes.

#### Validation Record

Test plans, results, feedback, defects, resolutions, approvals, and remaining limitations.

#### Reflection Record

Learner-authored interpretation of growth, difficulty, error, judgment, changed assumptions, and future action.

#### Handoff Record

Setup, operation, maintenance, known issues, rights, ownership, support, migration, and archive information needed by successors.

### Documentation Principles

- Capture knowledge close to the event that produced it
- Connect records to project versions and artifacts
- Preserve authorship and distinguish AI assistance
- Support structured data and human-readable export
- Keep source evidence accessible from summaries
- Allow private, team, evaluator, portfolio, and public visibility levels
- Protect sensitive research, personal information, and confidential work
- Mark draft, reviewed, approved, superseded, and archived status
- Retain meaningful history when current documentation changes
- Make documentation useful to the learner and future builder

> "Documentation preserves what creation teaches."

## 7. Portfolio Generation

Portfolio generation should help learners curate authentic evidence from the project rather than manufacture a polished story disconnected from the work.

### Portfolio Evidence Model

A portfolio entry should be able to show:

- **Purpose:** the problem, opportunity, audience, and intended value
- **Role:** what the learner owned, decided, created, and collaborated on
- **Process:** research, plan, architecture, iterations, and feedback
- **Artifact:** the selected outcome or demonstrable work
- **Evidence:** tests, rubric results, reviews, usage, or other validation
- **Capability:** skills and judgment credibly demonstrated
- **Reflection:** what the learner learned, changed, and would do next
- **Contribution:** value created for others and reusable knowledge left behind
- **Provenance:** sources, collaborators, tools, AI assistance, and verification

### Generation Workflow

```text
Project Records
  ↓
Learner Selects Evidence
  ↓
System Suggests Narrative Structure
  ↓
Learner Authors and Verifies Claims
  ↓
Reviewer Validates Where Required
  ↓
Portfolio Entry Created
  ↓
Learner Controls Sharing and Export
```

AI may suggest evidence and draft structure, but the learner should choose what represents the work and verify every claim. Team projects must distinguish individual and shared contribution.

Portfolio entries should link back to verifiable evidence where permissions allow and clearly mark claims that are self-reported, educator-reviewed, system-verified, or externally validated.

> "Every project should tell the story of a learner becoming a builder."

## 8. Publication Workflow

Publication is a governed transition from private or collaborative creation to an audience with defined expectations and consequences.

### Publication States

```text
Private Draft
  ↓
Review Requested
  ↓
Changes Required or Approved
  ↓
Release Candidate
  ↓
Published
  ↓
Updated, Corrected, Withdrawn, or Archived
```

### Readiness Review

Before publication, the workspace should evaluate:

- Purpose and intended audience
- Completion against requirements
- Factual accuracy and evidence
- Test and review status
- Accessibility and format quality
- Privacy, consent, and sensitive information
- Security and harmful-content risk
- Copyright, licensing, attribution, and collaborator approval
- Assessment and academic-integrity requirements
- Version, ownership, support, and correction process

Approval requirements should be proportional to context. A learner sharing a private mentor draft differs from publishing organizational content or executable software to the public.

Publication should create a stable version and preserve the source project. Later changes should produce version history, correction notes, or a new release as appropriate. Withdrawal should remove access where required without erasing necessary audit or learning records.

The learner should understand who can see the work, under what identity, with which license or terms, and how to change or end publication.

## 9. Product Implications

### Make Projects a Core Learning Mode

Projects should be reachable from learning paths, lessons, journeys, dashboards, assessments, and communities—not treated as optional attachments after content.

### Use One Project Identity across Domains

Build owns the project workspace. Learn may assign it, Prove may assess its evidence, Grow may interpret its capabilities, and Community may support collaboration without duplicating the record.

### Adapt Structure to Project Complexity

Templates and progressive disclosure should make the eight modules useful for a short individual project and a long multidisciplinary team effort.

### Preserve Authorship and Contribution

The product should record individual and shared work, source provenance, AI assistance, feedback, approvals, and version history without turning creative work into invasive surveillance.

### Make the Next Action Clear

Every module should show current phase, relevant state, blockers, and the most meaningful next action while allowing the learner to inspect the larger lifecycle.

### Integrate Feedback with Revision

Comments, rubrics, test findings, mentor guidance, and review requests should connect to artifact versions and resolution state.

### Support External Tools without Losing Knowledge

Learners may build in code hosts, design tools, drives, media systems, laboratories, or physical environments. LearningOS should link, import, synchronize, or reference external artifacts while preserving project context and evidence.

### Design Safe Collaboration

Roles, invitations, permissions, attribution, moderation, activity visibility, conflict handling, and handoffs should be understandable and appropriate to learner age and context.

### Separate Completion, Assessment, Portfolio, and Publication

A project may be complete without being assessed, assessed without being public, and included in a private portfolio without public publication. The interface should not collapse these distinct decisions.

## 10. Engineering Implications

### Model Projects as Durable Aggregates

A project should have stable identity, type, owner, organization, journey relationships, lifecycle state, capabilities, evidence requirements, permissions, and version history.

### Use Modular Bounded Contexts

Research, planning, artifacts, testing, documentation, portfolio projection, and publication may evolve independently but should communicate through explicit project contracts and events.

### Separate Mutable Work from Immutable Evidence

Drafts may change continuously. Submitted, assessed, portfolio, and published evidence should reference immutable snapshots or content-addressed versions where appropriate.

### Build an Artifact Abstraction

The workspace should support native and external files, documents, code repositories, links, media, structured records, and physical-project evidence through typed metadata, provenance, permissions, and preview capabilities.

### Implement Event and Audit History

Material state transitions, submissions, reviews, approvals, publications, permission changes, and AI actions should be attributable and timestamped. Auditability should respect privacy and avoid excessive behavioral capture.

### Design Collaboration for Concurrency

Editing, comments, tasks, reviews, presence, conflicts, and handoffs need explicit concurrency, ownership, notification, and recovery behavior.

### Enforce Server-Side Authorization

Access should account for user, team, organization, role, project state, artifact sensitivity, assessment rules, and publication status. Interface visibility is not authorization.

### Orchestrate Long-Running Work Reliably

Imports, exports, builds, tests, AI analysis, portfolio generation, media processing, and publication may require queued, retryable, observable jobs with idempotency and cancellation.

### Preserve Documentation Provenance

Structured records should link to sources, project versions, authors, AI generation, approvals, and superseding versions. Exports should remain understandable outside LearningOS.

### Engineer Publication as a Release System

Publication requires release candidates, checks, approvals, immutable versions, stable identifiers, visibility policies, rollback or withdrawal, corrections, and archival.

### Test the Complete Lifecycle

Verification should cover template creation, research provenance, planning revision, artifact versioning, collaboration, testing, feedback resolution, submission, reassessment, portfolio claims, publication, correction, withdrawal, export, deletion, and archive.

### Protect Reliability and Learner Work

Autosave, offline tolerance where practical, conflict recovery, backups, tested restoration, clear sync state, and graceful external-service failure are foundational. Lost learner work is a loss of trust and evidence.

## Canonical Status

The Project Workspace is Canonical Blueprint 017 and the authoritative Project Genesis reference for the complete project creation environment used throughout LearningOS.

Future project templates, modules, AI assistance, artifact systems, documentation, assessment connections, portfolio generation, collaboration, and publication workflows should be evaluated by whether they transform knowledge into authentic evidence and preserve the story of a learner becoming a builder.
