## Purpose and Authority

The Learner Journey Engine is the coordinating system that connects what a person hopes to become with the knowledge, practice, projects, evidence, and opportunities required to move toward it. It transforms separate LearningOS features into a coherent developmental journey.

The engine does not dictate one permanent path or reduce a person's future to an algorithmic prediction. It maintains shared journey context, reveals meaningful options, explains dependencies, adapts to evidence, and helps the learner decide and take the next responsible step.

This blueprint defines the journey model, stages, state, dashboard responsibilities, AI Mentor role, maps, and product and engineering requirements through which LearningOS guides aspiration toward demonstrated capability and continued growth.

> "People don't complete courses. They complete journeys."

---

## 1. Why Journeys Matter

People rarely begin learning because they want completion data. They begin with a desire: to understand, solve, create, qualify, change direction, serve, lead, or become capable of something that matters.

A course may contribute to that desire, but a course is only one possible part of the journey. Knowledge must connect to practice. Practice must connect to application. Application must produce evidence. Evidence should open reflection, contribution, and opportunity.

Journey design matters because it:

- Connects daily learning to personally meaningful aspiration
- Makes prerequisites, dependencies, and progress understandable
- Coordinates experiences across Learn, Build, Prove, Grow, and Community
- Preserves continuity across sessions, courses, projects, and institutions
- Adapts when evidence reveals a gap or a goal changes
- Helps educators and mentors understand where support is needed
- Prevents completion from being mistaken for capability
- Connects demonstrated capability to portfolios and real opportunity
- Makes the next step visible without hiding the larger destination
- Supports lifelong return, redirection, and continued development

A journey should be directional without becoming rigid. The learner may explore, pause, revisit foundations, change goals, bring prior experience, or pursue more than one destination over time.

> "Learning has direction."

## 2. The Journey Engine

The Journey Engine continuously relates learner purpose, current state, available pathways, evidence, constraints, and opportunities.

```text
Aspiration and Context
          ↓
Goal and Capability Definition
          ↓
Journey Map and Current State
          ↓
Available Actions and Dependencies
          ↓
Next-Step Recommendation
          ↓
Learner Decision and Action
          ↓
Evidence, Feedback, and Reflection
          ↓
Updated State and Adapted Journey
          ↺
```

### Engine Inputs

- Learner-stated dreams, goals, interests, and constraints
- Existing knowledge, skills, evidence, and credentials
- Enrollments, learning history, practice, and project activity
- Assessment results and feedback
- Availability, deadlines, access, roles, and organization context
- Learner preferences and accessibility needs
- Educator, mentor, program, and credential requirements
- Current pathways, resources, projects, and opportunities

### Engine Responsibilities

- Translate aspirations into reviewable goals and capabilities
- Identify relevant journey maps and possible routes
- Determine current journey state from credible evidence
- Resolve prerequisites and expose dependencies
- Rank meaningful next-step candidates
- Explain why a step is recommended and what it enables
- Detect blockers, risk, inactivity, conflict, and missing evidence
- Adapt the path when progress, goals, or conditions change
- Coordinate transitions among LearningOS domains
- Preserve history, decisions, and learner control

### Engine Outputs

- Current stage and journey position
- A small set of recommended next steps
- Progress narrative and supporting evidence
- Upcoming milestones, dependencies, and risks
- Adapted pathway options
- Mentor, educator, peer, and resource connections
- Portfolio and opportunity readiness signals

The engine should distinguish fact from inference. A completed lesson is a recorded fact; a predicted skill gap is an inference that requires explanation and may require validation.

## 3. The Five Journey Stages

### Stage I — Aspire

The learner identifies a dream, question, role, problem, contribution, or possible future that gives development meaning.

**Primary outcomes:** an articulated aspiration, relevant context, initial motivation, and questions worth exploring.

### Stage II — Plan

The aspiration becomes one or more reviewable goals, defined capabilities, evidence expectations, constraints, and a provisional learning path.

**Primary outcomes:** a chosen goal, capability map, journey map, starting point, milestones, and an understood first step.

### Stage III — Develop

The learner acquires knowledge and develops capability through lessons, practice, feedback, reflection, and progressively authentic application.

**Primary outcomes:** growing understanding, practiced skills, corrected misconceptions, stronger judgment, and readiness to build.

### Stage IV — Demonstrate

The learner creates projects, completes assessments, responds to feedback, and produces credible evidence that capability can be applied.

**Primary outcomes:** completed work, validated evidence, reflection, credentials where appropriate, and portfolio-ready artifacts.

### Stage V — Advance

The learner uses demonstrated capability to contribute, pursue opportunity, mentor others, deepen expertise, or choose a new aspiration.

**Primary outcomes:** portfolio communication, career or contribution pathways, continued growth, and a renewed journey.

The five stages organize the canonical journey:

```text
Dream
  ↓
Goal
  ↓
Learning Path
  ↓
Lessons
  ↓
Practice
  ↓
Projects
  ↓
Assessment
  ↓
Portfolio
  ↓
Career
```

The relationship between stages and journey elements is:

```text
ASPIRE          PLAN                 DEVELOP              DEMONSTRATE          ADVANCE
Dream     →     Goal     →     Learning Path     →     Lessons + Practice
                                                       ↓
Career    ←   Portfolio  ←     Assessment       ←     Projects
  ↺
New aspiration, deeper capability, contribution, or mentorship
```

Career represents one form of meaningful opportunity, not the only valid destination. A journey may advance toward independent creation, service, further education, entrepreneurship, research, community leadership, personal mastery, or another learner-defined contribution.

> "Every lesson should move the learner toward becoming someone new."

## 4. The Journey Dashboard

The Journey Dashboard is the focused view of one learner journey. The general Learner Dashboard orients the person across all active work; the Journey Dashboard explains the destination, map, present state, evidence, and next actions for a specific aspiration or goal.

### Journey Dashboard Regions

1. **Journey Identity** — aspiration, goal, intended capabilities, and meaningful purpose
2. **Current Position** — stage, active milestone, status, and recent movement
3. **Next Meaningful Step** — prioritized action, reason, expected outcome, and effort
4. **Journey Map** — completed, active, optional, blocked, and future nodes
5. **Progress Story** — evidence of growth, feedback, reflection, and capability change
6. **Active Work** — current lessons, practice, projects, assessments, and submissions
7. **Dependencies and Attention** — prerequisites, deadlines, blockers, and decisions
8. **People and Support** — AI Mentor, educators, human mentors, peers, and communities
9. **Portfolio and Opportunity** — evidence readiness, missing proof, and potential destinations

The dashboard should make one primary next action clear while allowing the learner to inspect alternatives and the larger journey. It should not compress a long developmental process into a single percentage.

### Multiple Journeys

A learner may maintain multiple journeys. LearningOS should distinguish:

- One primary journey when the learner chooses one
- Active secondary journeys
- Exploratory journeys not yet committed
- Paused journeys intended for return
- Completed or transitioned journeys retained in history

Shared learning, projects, or evidence may contribute to more than one journey without duplicating the canonical record.

## 5. AI Mentor Responsibilities

The AI Mentor helps the learner understand and navigate the journey while preserving human agency, accountable assessment, and appropriate human support.

### The AI Mentor Should

- Help clarify aspirations without imposing a life direction
- Translate broad goals into capabilities and achievable milestones
- Explain journey maps, prerequisites, and alternative routes
- Recommend next steps grounded in current state and learner goals
- Explain which information and reasoning informed a recommendation
- Adapt explanations, examples, and practice to learner needs
- Ask questions that reveal understanding and uncertainty
- Help learners plan projects and respond to feedback
- Support reflection and recognize patterns over time
- Identify blockers and connect the learner to appropriate support
- Prepare learners for assessment without manufacturing evidence
- Help organize portfolio narratives from authentic work
- Recognize when the journey should be reviewed or changed

### The AI Mentor Must Not

- Present a predicted future as destiny
- Make consequential career, credential, or admission decisions without accountable authority
- Infer sensitive attributes unnecessarily
- Hide uncertainty or fabricate progress
- Complete meaningful work in ways that prevent capability development
- Override educator requirements or validated evidence
- Manipulate engagement through pressure, shame, or false urgency
- Replace human support where safety, accommodation, judgment, or care requires it

The AI Mentor should know the learner's *journey context* only within clear permission and data-use boundaries. Learners should be able to inspect, correct, limit, export, and appropriately delete personal journey information.

## 6. Journey State

Journey State is the durable, explainable record of where a learner is, what has occurred, what evidence exists, what requires attention, and which actions are available.

### State Categories

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Identity | Journey ID, learner, organization, title, purpose, created date |
| Goal | Intended outcome, capabilities, target evidence, priority, timeframe |
| Position | Current stage, milestone, active node, completed nodes |
| Activity | Lessons, practice, projects, assessments, mentorship, reflection |
| Evidence | Artifacts, scores, feedback, demonstrations, credentials, provenance |
| Dependencies | Prerequisites, required reviews, access, deadlines, decisions |
| Support | AI Mentor context, educators, mentors, peers, accommodations |
| Recommendation | Candidate actions, rank, rationale, confidence, expiration |
| History | State transitions, goal revisions, pauses, resumptions, overrides |

### Journey Status

A journey may be:

- **Exploring:** aspiration is being clarified
- **Planned:** goal and initial map are accepted
- **Active:** the learner is progressing through available work
- **Blocked:** a dependency prevents the next required action
- **At Risk:** evidence suggests the plan or commitment needs review
- **Paused:** progress is intentionally suspended with context preserved
- **Completed:** the defined goal and evidence requirements are satisfied
- **Transitioned:** the journey has led into a new goal or opportunity
- **Withdrawn:** the learner has ended the journey without erasing its history

State transitions should be event-driven, timestamped, attributable, and explainable. High-impact changes such as verified capability, credential completion, or program failure require authoritative rules and appropriate human oversight.

Derived state should never silently overwrite source evidence. Corrections, overrides, and goal changes should preserve history and state why the interpretation changed.

## 7. Journey Maps

A Journey Map is a versioned graph of possible movement from aspiration to demonstrated capability and opportunity. It is not merely a list of courses.

### Journey Map Components

- **Destination:** the aspiration, goal, role, contribution, or capability sought
- **Capabilities:** the knowledge, skills, judgment, and behaviors required
- **Nodes:** lessons, resources, practice, projects, assessments, reflection, and support
- **Edges:** prerequisite, recommended, optional, alternative, or evidence relationships
- **Milestones:** meaningful intermediate outcomes
- **Evidence requirements:** what must be demonstrated and how it is validated
- **Choice points:** places where the learner selects among valid directions
- **Support points:** mentor, educator, peer, accessibility, or organizational help
- **Opportunity links:** career, project, community, entrepreneurship, or advanced learning destinations
- **Revision metadata:** owner, version, evidence, effective date, and change history

### Map Types

- **Exploration Map:** helps a learner understand possible goals before commitment
- **Capability Map:** organizes the abilities required for a defined outcome
- **Program Map:** represents an institutionally governed sequence and requirements
- **Personal Journey Map:** adapts a map to prior evidence, goals, and constraints
- **Recovery Map:** provides a responsible route around a blocker or capability gap
- **Transition Map:** connects demonstrated capability to opportunity or deeper study

Maps should support alternative pathways when different experiences can produce equivalent credible evidence. Personalization may change route, pace, support, and examples, but should not silently change the integrity of a defined capability or credential.

Journey authors should review maps using learner outcomes, evidence, accessibility, completion patterns, failures, and community experience. Superseded maps should remain traceable for learners whose records depend on them.

## 8. Product Implications

### Begin with Aspiration, Not Inventory

Discovery should help people express what they want to become or contribute before presenting an undifferentiated catalog of content.

### Connect Every Object to a Journey Purpose

Courses, lessons, practice, projects, assessments, and recommendations should indicate which capability, milestone, or destination they support.

### Preserve Learner Agency

Learners should be able to accept, defer, replace, or question recommendations; change goals; pause journeys; and understand the consequences of required program rules.

### Make the Next Step Explainable

Every recommended action should state what to do, why it matters now, what it enables, which evidence informed it, and what alternatives exist.

### Design Cross-Domain Continuity

Transitions among Learn, Build, Prove, Grow, and Community should carry journey identity and context without duplicating canonical records.

### Represent Progress as Evidence

Progress should combine completed journey nodes with capability evidence, feedback, project quality, reflection, and readiness—not rely on content completion alone.

### Support Return and Redirection

The experience should help learners resume after inactivity, recover from blockers, incorporate prior learning, and choose a new destination without treating change as failure.

### Connect Capability to Opportunity Responsibly

Portfolio and career suggestions should explain fit, uncertainty, prerequisites, and data sources. They should widen exploration rather than narrow a person's future through unsupported prediction.

> "The platform should always know the learner's next step."

The platform should interpret this principle as an obligation to maintain a current, explainable next-step candidate—or explicitly state what information or decision is needed before one can be known responsibly.

## 9. Engineering Implications

### Model Journeys as Versioned Graphs

Journey maps should support nodes, typed edges, alternatives, prerequisites, evidence requirements, milestones, and version history rather than hard-coded linear sequences.

### Separate Definition from Learner State

A versioned journey definition describes possible structure. A learner journey instance records personal goals, path choices, activity, evidence, and transitions against a specific definition version.

### Use Event-Derived State

Enrollment, completion, practice, submission, feedback, verification, reflection, pause, and goal-change events should update journey projections through idempotent, auditable processing.

### Preserve Canonical Domain Ownership

The Journey Engine references learning, project, assessment, portfolio, community, and account objects through stable identifiers. It should not become a duplicate source of truth for every domain.

### Build Explainable Recommendation Contracts

Recommendation outputs should include candidate action, supporting factors, excluded constraints, confidence or uncertainty, map version, creation time, expiration, and a human-readable rationale.

### Enforce Authoritative Requirements

Credential prerequisites, assessment validity, organization policies, and permissions require deterministic enforcement and accountable override mechanisms. AI recommendations cannot silently bypass them.

### Protect Privacy and Boundaries

Journey data may reveal ambitions, performance, difficulties, and career interests. Systems should minimize collection, enforce purpose-based access, support consent and retention rules, and prevent cross-organization leakage.

### Design for Recovery

Event replay, projection rebuilds, map migration, stale recommendations, partial service failure, conflicting updates, offline actions, and human correction need explicit recovery behavior.

### Instrument Journey Outcomes

Observability should cover transition success, blocked states, recommendation acceptance and usefulness, map dead ends, time to meaningful value, evidence attainment, redirection, and opportunity outcomes while avoiding surveillance metrics.

### Test Complete Journeys

Verification should include aspiration capture, map assignment, prior evidence, alternative paths, domain transitions, blockers, reassessment, pause and resume, map revision, portfolio creation, and opportunity transition.

## 10. Future Expansion

The Learner Journey Engine should develop toward a lifelong, portable, and human-governed system of capability growth.

Future expansion may include:

- Multi-year and lifelong journey continuity
- Portable skill and evidence records across institutions
- Recognition of prior learning and external evidence
- Richer alternative-path and equivalency models
- Mentor-authored and community-authored journey maps
- Institutional pathway exchange and interoperability
- Labor-market and contribution opportunity connections
- Team, entrepreneurship, research, and civic journey destinations
- Adaptive practice informed by longitudinal evidence
- Simulation of journey alternatives with transparent assumptions
- Multilingual and culturally contextualized maps
- Federated credentials and verifiable portfolio evidence
- Learner-controlled personal AI with portable journey memory
- Intergenerational pathways from learner to builder, mentor, educator, and steward

Expansion should preserve the permanent principles: aspiration belongs to the person; recommendations require explanation; capability requires evidence; consequential decisions require accountability; and every journey should expand rather than constrain human possibility.

The engine's long-term success is not the number of pathways completed. It is the number of people who can transform aspiration into capability, use capability to create meaningful value, and begin the next journey with greater agency.
