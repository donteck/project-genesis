## Purpose and Authority

The Portfolio & Evidence System connects what a learner has experienced with what they can credibly demonstrate. It gathers evidence from lessons, practice, projects, assessments, feedback, reflections, certifications, and contribution, then helps the learner curate that evidence into an honest and useful professional story.

A portfolio is not a decorative profile or a claim generator. It is a learner-controlled presentation built from attributable records. It should reveal capability, process, judgment, growth, and contribution while respecting privacy, authorship, collaboration, assessment integrity, and the limits of available evidence.

This blueprint defines the evidence model, seven portfolio sections, longitudinal timeline, AI Portfolio Coach, visibility, Living Resume, and product and engineering requirements through which capability becomes understandable and actionable opportunity.

> "Learning without evidence is difficult to demonstrate."

---

## 1. Why Evidence Matters

People often possess more capability than a title, transcript, or completion record can communicate. They may have solved problems, built useful artifacts, improved through feedback, contributed to teams, taught others, or developed judgment that remains invisible to conventional summaries.

Evidence matters because it:

- Makes capability observable and reviewable
- Connects claims to authentic work
- Distinguishes participation from demonstrated growth
- Shows how a learner thinks, builds, tests, and improves
- Preserves individual contribution within collaborative work
- Gives assessments and credentials understandable context
- Helps learners recognize patterns in their own development
- Creates material for mentorship and opportunity conversations
- Supports portable recognition across organizations and roles
- Allows others to evaluate fit without relying only on pedigree

Evidence does not speak entirely for itself. It needs provenance, criteria, context, ownership, and interpretation. A polished artifact may conceal how it was produced; an imperfect artifact may reveal important capability when its decisions and growth are visible.

The system should help learners make warranted claims: no weaker than the evidence deserves, and no stronger than the evidence can support.

## 2. The Portfolio Philosophy

The LearningOS portfolio is governed by the following principles.

### Evidence before Assertion

Capability claims should link to projects, assessments, demonstrations, feedback, credentials, or other credible records.

### Story with Provenance

A portfolio should explain purpose, process, role, decisions, outcomes, and growth while preserving sources, versions, collaborators, tools, and validation.

### Growth, Not Perfection

Strong portfolios may include drafts, failures, revisions, and changed understanding when those records reveal learning and judgment.

### Learner Agency

The learner controls which eligible evidence becomes part of a portfolio, how it is arranged, who may see it, and which personal narrative accompanies it, subject to lawful and institutional requirements.

### Honest Collaboration

Team work should distinguish shared outcome from individual contribution and recognize collaborators appropriately.

### Transparent AI Assistance

AI may help organize, explain, and improve presentation, but it should not invent experience, inflate outcomes, or overwrite the learner's authorship.

### Audience Relevance

One evidence base may support different views for an educator, mentor, employer, client, collaborator, institution, or community. Selective presentation should not alter the underlying truth.

### Living Continuity

The portfolio should evolve as capability grows. Older evidence remains part of the learner's history even when newer work becomes more representative.

> "Your portfolio tells the story that your résumé cannot."

## 3. The Evidence Model

Evidence is an attributable record that supports a claim about activity, growth, contribution, or demonstrated capability.

### Evidence Sources

- Lesson and practice responses
- Project artifacts and version history
- Research, plans, decisions, and architecture
- Test results and validation records
- Assessment responses and rubric outcomes
- Human observations and verified demonstrations
- Peer, mentor, educator, client, or stakeholder feedback
- Learner reflections and self-assessment
- Certificates, badges, transcripts, and external credentials
- Publications, presentations, contributions, and impact records

### Evidence Record

Every evidence item should be able to identify:

- Stable identity and evidence type
- Learner and relevant contributors
- Source object and canonical version
- Capability or claim supported
- Context, conditions, and intended criteria
- Authorship and individual contribution
- AI, tools, templates, and assistance used
- Evaluator, issuer, or verification authority
- Creation, observation, assessment, and update dates
- Visibility, consent, rights, and retention
- Current status, validity, and superseding evidence
- Limitations, uncertainty, or unresolved dispute

### Evidence Strength

Evidence strength depends on fit between claim and record, not on appearance alone.

| Strength dimension | Core question |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Does the evidence directly support the capability claimed? |
| Authenticity | Is the work attributable to the learner under understood conditions? |
| Quality | Does the evidence meet defined criteria or demonstrate meaningful value? |
| Validity | Does the assessment or observation measure what it claims to measure? |
| Provenance | Can origin, versions, contributors, tools, and review be traced? |
| Recency | Is the evidence current enough for the claim being made? |
| Transfer | Has capability appeared across contexts or under authentic conditions? |
| Verification | Has an accountable person, institution, or system validated the record? |

Learner reflection is valuable evidence of interpretation and growth, but it is not independent verification. System activity is evidence that an event occurred, but not automatically that learning occurred. Credentials summarize governed claims, but their underlying criteria and evidence determine their strength.

## 4. The Seven Portfolio Sections

### Section I — Identity & Direction

Introduces who the learner is becoming and what kind of work, problems, or contribution matter to them.

It may include:

- Name and professional identity
- Headline and concise introduction
- Current direction, interests, and goals
- Values or working principles
- Preferred roles, domains, or contribution types
- Contact and opportunity preferences

The learner should not be required to disclose sensitive personal information to have a credible portfolio.

### Section II — Capability Profile

Summarizes demonstrated knowledge, skills, judgment, and working capabilities with links to supporting evidence.

It may include:

- Capability name and level
- Evidence strength and recency
- Contexts in which capability was demonstrated
- Related projects, assessments, and credentials
- Current growth areas
- Verification status

### Section III — Featured Projects

Presents selected projects as complete stories of purpose, process, evidence, outcome, and growth.

Each project may include:

- Problem, audience, and intended value
- Learner role and collaborators
- Research, planning, and key decisions
- Selected artifact and meaningful iterations
- Tests, feedback, results, and limitations
- Capabilities demonstrated
- Reflection and future improvements

### Section IV — Credentials & Evidence

Collects certificates, badges, assessment results, endorsements, demonstrations, and verified records without presenting every credential as equivalent.

It should show issuer, criteria, capability level, dates, status, verification, and links to evidence where appropriate.

### Section V — Process & Reflection

Shows how the learner learns and improves.

It may include decision records, before-and-after artifacts, feedback responses, failures, revised assumptions, learning journals, and selected reflections approved for the audience.

### Section VI — Experience & Contribution

Shows how capability has created value with and for other people.

It may include team roles, work experience, community projects, mentorship, teaching, open knowledge, service, publications, events, and outcomes with clear attribution.

### Section VII — Growth & Next Direction

Connects past evidence to future development without treating an algorithmic prediction as destiny.

It may include current goals, capabilities under development, planned projects, requested mentorship, opportunity interests, and a learner-authored statement of the next meaningful direction.

The seven sections are canonical responsibilities, not mandatory public tabs. A portfolio view may combine, reorder, or omit sections according to audience, evidence, and learner choice while preserving honest claims.

> "Every project is a chapter in your professional journey."

## 5. The Evidence Timeline

The Evidence Timeline is the chronological view of how capability developed across learning, practice, creation, assessment, reflection, and opportunity.

The canonical evidence flow is:

```text
Lesson
  ↓
Practice
  ↓
Project
  ↓
Assessment
  ↓
Reflection
  ↓
Evidence
  ↓
Portfolio
  ↓
Career
```

### Timeline Events

The timeline may include:

- Journey or goal established
- Lesson or learning milestone completed
- Practice attempt and improvement pattern
- Project created, revised, tested, and completed
- Feedback received and resolved
- Assessment submitted, reviewed, appealed, or reassessed
- Capability demonstrated or credential issued
- Reflection approved for preservation
- Evidence added to or removed from a portfolio view
- Portfolio shared, exported, or presented
- Opportunity, contribution, or new journey connected

### Timeline Principles

- Preserve original dates and source records
- Distinguish activity from demonstrated capability
- Show meaningful change without displaying every low-value event
- Allow filtering by journey, project, capability, evidence type, and audience
- Keep corrections and superseding evidence traceable
- Respect visibility and organization boundaries
- Let learners add context without rewriting verified history
- Support private reflection and selective portfolio presentation

The timeline should make growth legible. It can reveal that a learner struggled, practiced, changed approach, completed a project, demonstrated capability, and carried that capability into opportunity.

Career represents one possible continuation. The timeline may also lead toward entrepreneurship, further education, research, service, mentorship, personal mastery, or another form of contribution.

## 6. AI Portfolio Coach

The AI Portfolio Coach helps learners identify, organize, explain, and present authentic evidence. It is a role of the LearningOS AI Mentor governed by the same rules for agency, memory, truth, privacy, and assessment integrity.

### Appropriate Responsibilities

The Coach may:

- Identify eligible evidence from learner-approved records
- Suggest which artifacts best support a stated capability
- Detect unsupported, duplicated, outdated, or conflicting claims
- Ask questions that help the learner explain purpose and role
- Suggest a project case-study structure
- Help compare drafts and reveal growth
- Improve clarity, grammar, accessibility, and audience fit
- Map credentials and projects to capabilities
- Suggest portfolio views for different learner-chosen audiences
- Identify missing provenance, attribution, rights, or verification
- Prepare the learner to present or discuss the work
- Recommend a future project to strengthen weak evidence

### Required Boundaries

The Coach must not:

- Invent projects, roles, skills, outcomes, employers, clients, or endorsements
- Inflate a partial contribution into sole authorship
- Present generated language as an independently verified fact
- Hide AI assistance when disclosure is required
- publish or share evidence without appropriate learner action and consent
- expose private assessments, reflections, collaborators, or organization data
- optimize a portfolio through stereotypes or unsupported career predictions
- remove important limitations solely to make a claim more impressive

### Authorship Workflow

```text
Learner Chooses Purpose and Audience
  ↓
Coach Suggests Eligible Evidence
  ↓
Learner Selects Records
  ↓
Coach Proposes Structure or Draft
  ↓
Learner Edits and Verifies Every Claim
  ↓
Required Review or Consent Is Obtained
  ↓
Learner Publishes or Shares
```

The learner remains the author and final decision-maker. AI-generated suggestions should be distinguishable in editing history and should never alter source evidence.

## 7. Visibility & Privacy

Portfolio value depends on visibility, but visibility must remain deliberate, granular, revocable where possible, and appropriate to the evidence.

### Visibility Levels

- **Private:** visible only to the learner and explicitly authorized platform services
- **Mentor:** shared with selected mentors, educators, or reviewers
- **Team:** shared with project collaborators
- **Organization:** shared within an academy, employer, institution, or governed community
- **Link Restricted:** available through a controlled or expiring link
- **Public:** discoverable or accessible without authenticated membership

Visibility should apply to portfolio views and individual evidence. A public portfolio does not make every underlying record public.

### Privacy Principles

- Private by default for new evidence
- Clear preview of exactly what an audience will see
- Separate controls for profile, project, credential, reflection, feedback, and contact data
- Consent and attribution for collaborators and third-party material
- Protection of minors and other context-specific safeguards
- No exposure of accommodations, private feedback, assessment items, or sensitive analytics
- Revocable links and sharing where technically and legally possible
- Search indexing and download controls communicated honestly
- Defined retention and deletion behavior
- No reuse of portfolio content for model training without appropriate authority and disclosure

### Audience Views

The learner may create audience-specific views that select relevant evidence and narrative without changing canonical records. A career view, mentor view, academic view, client view, or public showcase may emphasize different capabilities.

Audience views should preserve truth. Hiding irrelevant evidence is curation; hiding material limitations in order to misrepresent a claim is not.

### Shared and Organizational Evidence

Organization-issued credentials, team artifacts, client work, and confidential projects may carry restrictions independent of learner preference. The system should explain these restrictions, support approval workflows, and allow privacy-preserving evidence where a full artifact cannot be shared.

## 8. The Living Resume

The Living Resume is a learner-controlled, evidence-linked professional summary generated from the current portfolio and Learning Passport. Unlike a static résumé, it can remain connected to verified capability, project history, credentials, and growth.

### Living Resume Components

- Professional identity and learner-authored summary
- Selected capabilities with evidence links
- Featured projects and individual contributions
- Experience, service, teaching, and community work
- Current and verified credentials
- Education and learning journeys
- Publications, presentations, or recognized contributions
- Opportunity preferences and contact controls

### Living Behavior

The Living Resume may:

- Suggest updates when new eligible evidence appears
- Flag claims whose supporting credentials expired or changed status
- Tailor selection and ordering to a learner-chosen opportunity
- Generate accessible web, print, and portable document views
- Link to verifiable credentials and portfolio case studies
- Preserve dated snapshots of submitted versions
- Compare a role description with evidence while explaining uncertainty
- Identify gaps that a future project or assessment could address

Automatic updates should never silently change a resume already submitted to an opportunity. The learner should review and approve every generated claim and audience-specific version.

### Relationship to the Learning Passport

The Learning Passport is the durable, privacy-aware record of verified learning and credentials. The portfolio is the curated evidence story. The Living Resume is the concise professional presentation selected from both.

```text
Learning Passport + Portfolio Evidence + Learner Narrative
                              ↓
                       Living Resume
                              ↓
                 Audience-Specific Snapshot
```

> "Capability becomes opportunity when it is visible."

## 9. Product Implications

### Capture Evidence during Learning

Lessons, practice, projects, assessments, feedback, and reflection should produce eligible evidence near the event rather than depend on reconstruction at portfolio time.

### Keep Curation Learner Controlled

The system may recommend evidence, but the learner chooses what appears, how it is described, and who may see it within applicable rights and program requirements.

### Show Claim-to-Evidence Relationships

Capability labels, resume statements, and portfolio narratives should make supporting projects, assessments, credentials, or verification available at an appropriate level of detail.

### Distinguish Evidence Status

Interfaces should communicate learner-authored, system-recorded, peer-reviewed, mentor-reviewed, institution-verified, externally verified, disputed, expired, and revoked states clearly.

### Make Team Contribution Honest

Project portfolios should show shared outcomes, learner role, collaborators, contribution evidence, and approvals required for public use.

### Support Progressive Portfolio Building

Early learners should be able to create credible small entries. Advanced learners should have deeper case studies, multiple audience views, verified credentials, and longitudinal capability narratives.

### Design for Presentation and Inspection

Portfolio views should work at two depths: a clear summary for quick understanding and traceable detail for reviewers who need evidence.

### Preserve Accessibility

Templates, media, exports, color, typography, navigation, alternative text, captions, document structure, and interaction should support accessible creation and consumption.

### Connect to Opportunity without Exploitation

Career and opportunity features should use learner-selected evidence, explain matching logic, avoid discriminatory proxies, and prevent unauthorized employer or third-party access.

### Avoid Portfolio Homogenization

Templates and AI should improve clarity without making every learner sound or look identical. The system should preserve voice, discipline-specific forms, and different kinds of contribution.

## 10. Engineering Implications

### Build an Evidence Graph

Model stable relationships among learner, journey, lesson, practice, project, artifact version, assessment, rubric, reflection, capability, credential, reviewer, portfolio entry, audience view, and opportunity snapshot.

### Preserve Canonical Ownership

Learn, Build, Prove, Grow, Community, and Account remain sources of truth for their objects. The Portfolio & Evidence System references immutable or versioned records rather than copying mutable content without provenance.

### Separate Source Evidence from Presentation

Evidence records and verification state should remain independent of portfolio layout, narrative, theme, resume format, and audience view. A presentation change must not alter the underlying claim.

### Use Immutable Snapshots for Shared Claims

Published portfolios, submitted resumes, assessed projects, and verified credentials should reference stable snapshots. Later corrections or updates should create traceable versions and status changes.

### Implement Fine-Grained Authorization

Authorization should consider learner, organization, evidence type, contributor, assessment policy, visibility level, audience link, credential status, and third-party rights. Public URLs must not bypass evidence-level restrictions.

### Protect Media and Downloads

Storage, transforms, previews, signed access, malware scanning, rights metadata, watermarking where appropriate, expiration, and deletion should match artifact sensitivity and audience.

### Engineer Verification

Credentials and reviewed claims need issuer authority, integrity protection, status resolution, stable identifiers, verification endpoints, expiration, correction, and revocation behavior.

### Make AI Suggestions Non-Destructive

AI Portfolio Coach outputs should be drafts or proposals with source references. They should never mutate canonical evidence, verification, publication, or resume-submission state without explicit authorized action.

### Support Search and Indexing Safely

Only evidence intended for the current audience should be indexed or embedded. Search deletion, visibility change, public cache, and external crawler behavior require explicit handling.

### Generate Portable Exports

Portfolios and Living Resumes should support accessible, durable exports with clear provenance and links where practical. Structured credential and evidence formats should use appropriate interoperability standards.

### Record Material Events

Evidence creation, verification, dispute, correction, portfolio publication, visibility change, link creation, export, resume snapshot, and credential status changes should be attributable and auditable.

### Observe with Privacy

Measure portfolio completion, evidence quality, verification success, sharing failures, accessibility, and opportunity outcomes without tracking recipient behavior beyond necessity and consent.

### Test the Full Evidence Lifecycle

Verification should cover evidence eligibility, team attribution, AI assistance, source updates, credential expiration, disputes, visibility combinations, restricted links, public removal, portfolio versions, resume snapshots, exports, cross-organization boundaries, and restoration.
