## Purpose and Authority

The Knowledge Cycle translates the Constitution of Learning into a durable pattern of action. It defines how a person encounters knowledge, develops capability, proves that capability through work, contributes what was learned, and improves the body of knowledge inherited by others.

This reconstruction restores the missing canonical position between Blueprint 001 and Blueprint 003. It does not replace either document: Blueprint 001 defines learning, this blueprint defines its regenerative cycle, and Blueprint 003 defines the civilization that emerges when generations live that cycle.

> "Knowledge fulfills its purpose when it becomes capability, contribution, and inheritance."

---

## 1. Why a Knowledge Cycle Matters

Knowledge that is only consumed remains fragile. Capability that is never demonstrated remains uncertain. Experience that is never reflected upon remains difficult to transfer. Work that is never preserved forces each generation to begin again.

LearningOS therefore treats knowledge as a living cycle rather than a static inventory. Every learning experience should help people move from understanding toward responsible action and return useful evidence, explanations, and improvements to the commons.

The cycle matters because it:

- Connects information to demonstrated capability
- Makes practice and feedback part of knowledge formation
- Preserves evidence of what a learner can do
- Turns individual insight into shared value
- Makes teaching and mentoring forms of deeper learning
- Carries context and lessons across generations
- Allows knowledge to be corrected as evidence changes
- Prevents content completion from becoming the definition of education

---

## 2. The Canonical Knowledge Cycle

```text
Discover
   ↓
Understand
   ↓
Practice
   ↓
Build
   ↓
Demonstrate
   ↓
Reflect
   ↓
Contribute
   ↓
Teach
   ↓
Preserve
   ↓
Renew
   ↺
```

**Discover** identifies a meaningful question, need, possibility, or body of knowledge. **Understand** forms a working mental model. **Practice** develops fluency through repeated application and feedback. **Build** integrates knowledge into useful work. **Demonstrate** produces reviewable evidence of capability. **Reflect** converts experience into judgment. **Contribute** makes the result useful to others. **Teach** tests and transfers understanding. **Preserve** retains the work, provenance, context, and lessons. **Renew** revises the inherited record when new evidence or conditions require change.

The stages are ordered but not rigid. Learners may return to earlier stages, repeat practice, revise a build, or discover a new question while teaching. What must remain intact is the movement from knowledge toward capability and from capability toward shared inheritance.

---

## 3. Cycle Invariants

Every implementation of the Knowledge Cycle must preserve these invariants:

1. Learning begins from purpose, curiosity, need, or possibility.
2. Understanding is tested through active use, not assumed from exposure.
3. Practice includes feedback and opportunity for revision.
4. Demonstration produces inspectable evidence.
5. Reflection records lessons, uncertainty, and changed judgment.
6. Contribution respects consent, attribution, privacy, and safety.
7. Preservation retains provenance and historical context.
8. Renewal changes knowledge through evidence and accountable review.
9. AI may assist every stage but may not falsely claim human capability.
10. The learner retains dignity, agency, and meaningful control.

---

## 4. Knowledge States

LearningOS should distinguish knowledge states rather than treating all material as equally authoritative.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Encountered | The learner has been exposed to an idea or method. |
| Understood | The learner can explain relationships and meaning. |
| Practiced | The learner has applied the knowledge with feedback. |
| Demonstrated | Evidence supports independent capability in context. |
| Contributed | The capability has produced value for others. |
| Preserved | The result and its provenance are available for responsible reuse. |
| Renewed | The record has been corrected, extended, or reinterpreted through new evidence. |

These states support guidance and reflection; they must not become labels that permanently limit a person.

---

## 5. Evidence and Provenance

Every meaningful cycle should leave a trustworthy trail: the learner's goal, resources used, practice attempts, feedback, project artifacts, assessment decisions, reflection, contributors, versions, and conditions under which the work was produced.

Evidence must remain proportional to the claim. A quiz can support a narrow recall claim; a reviewed project may support integrated capability; sustained contribution may support broader trust. Provenance must distinguish the learner's work from templates, collaborators, mentors, and AI assistance.

---

## 6. Contribution and Teaching

Contribution is not an optional final decoration. It is where capability encounters responsibility. A contribution may be a solution, explanation, dataset, lesson, tool, mentorship interaction, correction, or documented failure.

Teaching strengthens the cycle because explaining knowledge exposes gaps, requires structure, and creates pathways for others. Learners should be able to become mentors without being represented as experts beyond their demonstrated scope.

---

## 7. Preservation and Renewal

Preservation protects more than final answers. It should retain why a decision was made, which evidence supported it, who contributed, what limitations were known, and how the record changed.

Renewal is governed improvement, not silent replacement. Superseded knowledge should remain discoverable when lawful and safe, with its status and successor made clear. Canonical knowledge requires review authority; community knowledge requires transparent provenance and confidence.

---

## 8. AI in the Knowledge Cycle

AI may help learners discover resources, explain concepts, generate practice, provide feedback, organize evidence, prompt reflection, connect related knowledge, and prepare contributions. It must disclose material assistance, respect authorization boundaries, cite sources when claims depend on them, and preserve uncertainty.

AI output is not automatically knowledge and AI completion is not learner capability. Human judgment, evidence, review, and responsible use remain necessary.

---

## 9. Product and Engineering Implications

LearningOS should make the cycle visible through journeys, workspaces, feedback, portfolios, mentorship, content, knowledge graphs, and analytics. Systems should support resumable progress, versioned artifacts, evidence relationships, attribution, reflection, reuse permissions, and correction workflows.

Metrics should measure movement toward capability and contribution rather than optimize only for consumption, clicks, or time spent. Architecture should preserve domain ownership while allowing events and stable contracts to connect stages of the cycle.

---

## 10. Governance and Measurement

Cycle governance should answer:

- Who may publish, review, certify, correct, or retire knowledge?
- What evidence supports each claim?
- How are conflicts, appeals, and corrections resolved?
- Which contributions may be shared, and with whom?
- How are learner agency, privacy, attribution, and safety protected?

Healthy measures include demonstrated capability, quality of evidence, improvement after feedback, reuse with attribution, successful teaching transfer, correction latency, and durable contribution. No single metric should substitute for judgment.

## Permanent Principles

> "Knowledge fulfills its purpose when it becomes capability, contribution, and inheritance."

> "Learning is incomplete until understanding can survive responsible application."

> "Evidence makes capability trustworthy."

> "Teaching turns individual learning into shared possibility."

> "Preservation without renewal becomes stagnation; renewal without preservation becomes amnesia."
