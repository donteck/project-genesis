## Purpose and Authority

The Learner Dashboard is the primary orientation and action surface for every learner in LearningOS. It should help a person understand where they are, what has changed, how they are progressing, and which meaningful action deserves attention next.

The dashboard is not a collection of disconnected statistics or a promotional landing page. It is a personalized continuity layer that connects Learn, Build, Prove, Grow, and Community while preserving the learner's agency and attention.

This blueprint defines the dashboard promise, nine canonical zones, layout priorities, AI Mentor role, progress story, project integration, next-step logic, reflection system, and implementation responsibilities. Visual styling and responsive composition may evolve while preserving these purposes.

> "The dashboard should eliminate uncertainty."

---

## 1. Why the Dashboard Matters

Learning is rarely a single uninterrupted path. People return after hours, days, or months. They move among courses, projects, assessments, goals, mentors, communities, and obligations. Each return creates a moment of uncertainty: *Where was I? What matters now? What should I do next?*

The dashboard matters because it can:

- Restore context quickly after time away
- Make the most meaningful next action visible
- Connect activity to goals and demonstrated capability
- Show progress across learning, building, proving, and growth
- Surface important deadlines and risks without creating anxiety
- Help learners resume unfinished work
- Reveal patterns that support reflection and better decisions
- Connect learners to mentors, peers, and community support
- Celebrate meaningful milestones without manipulating engagement
- Reduce the effort required to navigate a complex learning system

A weak dashboard transfers system complexity to the learner. A strong dashboard organizes complexity around purpose, continuity, and action.

The dashboard should not attempt to display everything. Its success comes from selecting what matters now and providing clear paths to deeper domain views.

---

## 2. The Dashboard Promise

The Learner Dashboard makes this promise:

> **Know where you are. Understand your progress. Take your next meaningful step.**

The promise requires the dashboard to answer:

1. **Where am I now?** Which learning, projects, evidence, goals, and communities are active?
2. **What has changed?** What was completed, updated, reviewed, assigned, or newly available?
3. **How am I growing?** Which capabilities are strengthening, and where is support needed?
4. **What deserves attention?** Which deadlines, feedback, blockers, or commitments matter now?
5. **What should I do next?** Which action best advances the learner's stated purpose?
6. **Why this action?** What goal, dependency, evidence, or opportunity makes it relevant?
7. **Where can I get help?** Which mentor, peer, resource, or AI support is available?

The dashboard should provide useful answers immediately while allowing the learner to inspect reasoning, change goals, dismiss irrelevant suggestions, and enter the owning domain for detail.

> "Every learner deserves to know their next meaningful step."

## 3. The Nine Dashboard Zones

The canonical dashboard contains nine zones. Their relative size and placement may respond to screen, role, context, and learner state, but their responsibilities remain stable.

### Zone 1 — Welcome

Re-establish identity, context, purpose, and current moment. The zone includes a concise greeting, active organization or academy where relevant, and the most important orientation message.

### Zone 2 — Continue Learning

Provide direct resume access to the most relevant active lesson, course, learning path, or practice activity with accurate progress and preserved state.

### Zone 3 — Progress Story

Summarize meaningful growth across knowledge, practice, projects, evidence, goals, and contribution. Show trends and milestones with context rather than isolated percentages.

### Zone 4 — AI Mentor

Offer contextual guidance, questions, explanations, planning, reflection, and support grounded in permitted learner data and transparent reasoning.

### Zone 5 — Active Projects

Show current project state, next milestone, blockers, feedback, collaborators, and a direct path to resume building.

### Zone 6 — Recommended Next Steps

Prioritize a small number of explainable actions connected to learner goals, dependencies, progress, evidence, and opportunity.

### Zone 7 — Schedule & Attention

Surface deadlines, live sessions, reviews, expiring requirements, overdue commitments, and other time-sensitive items without turning the dashboard into a pressure system.

### Zone 8 — Community & Support

Show relevant mentor responses, cohort activity, peer requests, events, announcements, and safe ways to ask for help or contribute.

### Zone 9 — Reflection

Invite brief, purposeful reflection on learning, difficulty, confidence, decisions, and intended next action. Preserve reflections as part of the learner's growth record.

### Canonical Dashboard Layout

```text
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. WELCOME                                      | 7. SCHEDULE & ATTENTION       |
| Identity • Context • Purpose • Today's focus    | Deadlines • Events • Alerts   |
+-------------------------------------------------+------------------------------+
| 2. CONTINUE LEARNING                            | 4. AI MENTOR                  |
| Active path • Current lesson • Resume           | Guidance • Questions • Help  |
+-------------------------------------------------+------------------------------+
| 3. PROGRESS STORY                               | 6. RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS     |
| Growth • Skills • Milestones • Evidence         | Prioritized • Explained       |
+-------------------------------------------------+------------------------------+
| 5. ACTIVE PROJECTS                              | 8. COMMUNITY & SUPPORT        |
| Milestones • Feedback • Blockers • Resume       | Mentor • Cohort • Events      |
+-------------------------------------------------+------------------------------+
| 9. REFLECTION                                                                  |
| What did I learn? • What challenged me? • What will I do next?                 |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

The layout describes information priority, not fixed dimensions. Continue Learning and Recommended Next Steps should remain readily actionable. The dashboard should progressively reveal detail and link to canonical domain locations rather than reproducing complete domain workspaces.

## 4. Welcome Experience

The Welcome zone should help a learner feel recognized, oriented, and ready to act without wasting attention on decoration.

### Welcome Content

The zone may include:

- Preferred name and an appropriate time-aware greeting
- Active organization, academy, cohort, or personal context
- Current learning goal or purpose statement
- A concise summary of progress since the last visit
- A meaningful milestone or completed contribution
- One primary action when a clear priority exists
- Setup guidance for a new or incomplete account

Personalization should be purposeful and respectful. The dashboard should not infer sensitive personal states, use false intimacy, or reveal private information in shared environments.

### First-Visit Experience

A new learner should receive orientation rather than an empty dashboard. The experience should help them:

1. Understand what LearningOS can help them accomplish
2. Identify or choose a meaningful goal
3. Select or receive an appropriate starting path
4. Understand how Learn, Build, Prove, Grow, and Community connect
5. Take a small first action that creates momentum

### Returning Experience

A returning learner should see continuity: what they were doing, what changed, and what deserves attention. Promotional content should not displace active commitments or progress.

### Re-Entry after Inactivity

After a longer absence, the welcome should summarize prior context, identify changed deadlines or access, and offer a realistic re-entry plan. It should avoid shame-based messages or artificial urgency.

## 5. Progress Overview

The Progress Story should help learners interpret growth, not merely observe activity.

### Progress Dimensions

Progress may include:

- Knowledge understood and retained
- Practice completed and performance improved
- Projects created, revised, and completed
- Skills demonstrated through credible evidence
- Feedback received and acted upon
- Goals advanced or responsibly changed
- Credentials earned and linked to evidence
- Contributions made to peers or communities
- Reflection patterns and learning consistency

### Narrative Structure

The progress overview should answer:

- Where did I begin?
- What have I attempted?
- What can I now demonstrate?
- How has my work improved?
- Which evidence supports that conclusion?
- Where am I encountering difficulty?
- What is the next meaningful growth edge?

Percentages may be appropriate for bounded sequences such as course or project completion, but they should be labeled precisely. A course percentage is not a universal measure of learning, skill, or human potential.

Progress should compare learners primarily with their goals, prior evidence, and defined capability standards—not with arbitrary peer rankings. Social comparison requires a clear learning purpose, careful consent, and safeguards against distortion or harm.

> "Progress should tell a story, not merely display a percentage."

## 6. AI Mentor Integration

The AI Mentor should help the learner interpret context, make a plan, overcome difficulty, reflect, and act with increasing independence.

### Appropriate AI Mentor Roles

- Explain why a next step is recommended
- Clarify concepts using relevant examples
- Ask questions that reveal understanding
- Help break a goal or project into manageable steps
- Generate practice aligned with an intended capability
- Provide formative feedback on learner work
- Help interpret progress and feedback
- Support reflection and planning
- Suggest relevant human or community support
- Identify uncertainty, missing evidence, or a possible blocker

### Dashboard Behavior

The AI Mentor zone should begin with contextually useful prompts rather than a blank chat alone. It may reference current goals, active learning, projects, evidence, and upcoming commitments only when permissions and data-use expectations allow.

Recommendations should be explainable. Learners should be able to ask why, inspect the information used, correct inaccurate assumptions, dismiss suggestions, and control appropriate personalization.

The AI Mentor must not manufacture achievement, impersonate a human mentor, make unsupported high-stakes decisions, expose another person's information, or complete work in ways that prevent the intended learning.

Human escalation should be clear when a learner needs educator judgment, emotional or safety support, accessibility accommodation, account assistance, or another form of help beyond the AI Mentor's role.

## 7. Active Projects

The Active Projects zone connects learning to building and gives unfinished work a clear path back into motion.

Each project summary should show, when relevant:

- Project title, purpose, and owning Build workspace
- Current phase or milestone
- Most meaningful next action
- Progress grounded in defined deliverables
- Recent feedback and unresolved review items
- Blockers, dependencies, or decisions needed
- Collaborators, ownership, and pending handoffs
- Due dates or planned checkpoints
- Save, sync, draft, and submission state
- Evidence or capabilities the project can demonstrate

The zone should prioritize active and actionable projects rather than display an undifferentiated archive. Learners should be able to resume the current project directly and open Build for full project management.

Blocked projects deserve particular attention. The dashboard should identify the nature of the blocker, suggest a responsible path forward, and connect the learner to a resource, peer, mentor, educator, or decision owner where appropriate.

Completed projects should move into Prove, portfolios, reflection, or contribution pathways rather than disappear. The dashboard may celebrate completion briefly while preserving the artifact in its canonical home.

## 8. Recommended Next Steps

Recommended Next Steps should convert goals, progress, evidence, and context into a short, understandable set of possible actions.

### Recommendation Priorities

Recommendations should consider:

1. Safety, access, or integrity issues requiring resolution
2. Time-sensitive commitments and dependencies
3. Resume continuity for meaningful active work
4. Learner-selected goals and plans
5. Identified capability gaps or required practice
6. Feedback awaiting response
7. Opportunities to demonstrate or apply learning
8. Contribution, mentorship, or community opportunities
9. Exploration that may reveal a new direction

### Recommendation Requirements

Each recommendation should explain:

- **Action:** What the learner can do
- **Reason:** Why it matters now
- **Outcome:** What progress or capability it supports
- **Effort:** A useful estimate when reasonably knowable
- **Source:** Which goal, deadline, feedback, or system signal informed it
- **Control:** How to start, defer, dismiss, or change direction

The dashboard should present a small number of prioritized recommendations rather than a feed optimized for consumption. Recommendations must respect prerequisites, permissions, accessibility needs, organization rules, and learner preferences.

The learner remains the decision-maker. The system should learn from accepted, deferred, dismissed, and corrected suggestions without turning those choices into punitive signals.

## 9. Reflection System

Reflection helps learners convert activity and experience into transferable understanding. The dashboard provides a consistent, low-friction place to pause and interpret growth.

### Reflection Prompts

Prompts may ask:

- What did I understand more clearly today?
- What did I build, practice, or improve?
- What evidence shows progress?
- Where did I struggle, and what might explain it?
- Which feedback changed my approach?
- What assumption or question changed?
- What help do I need?
- What will I do next, and why?
- What could I teach or share with someone else?

### Reflection Principles

Reflection should be:

- Purposeful rather than repetitive
- Connected to actual learning, projects, evidence, or goals
- Private by default unless the learner chooses or a clearly disclosed program requires sharing
- Editable and exportable as part of the learner's record
- Accessible in multiple appropriate input formats
- Used to support learner insight, not covert behavioral judgment
- Summarized carefully with source access and correction controls when AI is involved

The system may identify themes over time, connect reflection to growth planning, and suggest earlier insights at relevant moments. It should distinguish the learner's words from AI interpretation.

Reflection is not a substitute for action. It should conclude with an insight, question, support need, or next step that the learner can carry forward.

## 10. Product & Engineering Implications

### Treat the Dashboard as an Orchestration Layer

The dashboard should aggregate summaries and actions from Learn, Build, Prove, Grow, Community, and Account while preserving canonical ownership in those domains.

### Define Zone Contracts

Each zone should have a documented purpose, data sources, permissions, loading behavior, empty state, error state, primary action, freshness expectation, and responsive priority.

### Build a Prioritization Service with Explainability

Next-step ranking should combine transparent rules and carefully governed models. The system should retain the factors behind a recommendation, support inspection, and avoid using sensitive attributes or manipulative engagement objectives.

### Preserve Learner Context

Resume actions should restore the correct organization, course, lesson, project, milestone, draft, assessment, or community context without losing work.

### Keep Domain Data Canonical

Dashboard cards should reference stable domain objects and identifiers. Editing through the dashboard must update the source of truth rather than create duplicate state.

### Design Independent Failure Boundaries

One unavailable zone should not make the entire dashboard unusable. Zones should provide scoped loading, stale-data indicators, retry behavior, and meaningful fallbacks.

### Protect Performance and Stability

The Welcome, Continue Learning, and Recommended Next Steps zones should become useful quickly. Secondary zones may load progressively without causing layout shift or focus disruption.

### Enforce Privacy and Authorization

Every query and action must respect identity, organization, role, enrollment, ownership, consent, and record sensitivity. Shared-device and notification previews should avoid exposing private learning information.

### Build Accessible Dashboard Semantics

Use one clear page heading, meaningful region labels, logical reading and focus order, keyboard-operable controls, understandable status, non-color indicators, and responsive layouts that preserve priority.

### Instrument Meaningful Outcomes

Measure orientation success, resume success, recommendation usefulness, task completion, help access, reflection value, progress understanding, and dead ends—not only card clicks or time spent.

### Test Learner States

Verification should cover new, active, returning, inactive, blocked, highly engaged, completed, multi-organization, offline, accessibility, and empty-data experiences across viewport sizes.

### Govern Dashboard Growth

New zones require evidence of a distinct learner need that cannot fit an existing zone. The dashboard must not become the default destination for every new feature or organizational message.

> "The best dashboard inspires action."

## Canonical Status

The Learner Dashboard is Canonical Blueprint 014 and the authoritative Project Genesis reference for the primary dashboard experience of every learner in LearningOS.

Future dashboard zones, progress summaries, AI Mentor experiences, project cards, recommendations, reflections, alerts, and responsive layouts should be evaluated by whether they eliminate uncertainty, tell an honest story of growth, and help every learner take a meaningful next step.
