# Genesis 9.8 — Enterprise Security Mesh, Zero Trust Architecture, Identity Fabric, Cyber Resilience & Digital Trust Framework

**Theme:** Embed security, privacy, resilience, and digital trust into every LearningOS interaction.

## Vision

Protect every user, application, device, AI agent, API, workflow, and dataset through continuous verification, least privilege, policy-driven access, resilient operations, and auditable governance.

## Primary Objectives

- Implement Zero Trust Architecture.
- Build the Enterprise Identity Fabric.
- Protect privileged identities, secrets, keys, data, and AI systems.
- Deliver continuous monitoring, detection, response, and recovery.
- Establish privacy engineering and digital trust governance.

## Major Platform Capabilities

Zero Trust controls include adaptive authentication, device posture, session risk, least privilege, micro-segmentation, contextual authorization, policy decisions, continuous validation, and immediate revocation.

The Identity Fabric manages learners, educators, staff, administrators, institutions, partners, service accounts, APIs, devices, and AI agents. Access is governed through roles, attributes, relationships, purpose, risk, and time-bound elevation.

Privileged Access Management provides approval, just-in-time access, session recording, emergency procedures, credential rotation, and review. Secrets and cryptographic materials are centrally protected and rotated.

Security Operations integrates logs, alerts, detections, cases, investigations, response playbooks, evidence, metrics, and executive reporting. Cyber resilience defines recovery objectives, backups, restoration, regional failover, continuity plans, and resilience tests.

Privacy engineering includes minimization, consent, classification, retention, subject-rights workflows, cross-border controls, and privacy impact assessments. AI security covers prompt injection, data leakage, model access, tool abuse, supply-chain risk, monitoring, and human escalation.
